# Spec 0002: Quiz v2, playstyle-led matching and a redesigned Question set

Status: Implemented on branch worktree-agent-ac77e5e8d632ad40e and evaluated. Proposed 2026-09-23; awaiting maintainer review before merge.
Date: 2026-09-23
Related: docs/research/2026-09-23-quiz-question-design.md, docs/research/2026-09-23-question-redesign.md, docs/adr/0004-playstyle-led-matching.md, docs/eval/test-players.md, docs/research/2026-09-23-quiz-v2-evaluation.md

## Problem Statement

We tested multi-Build Legend ratings with a few Players. One found their top Legend accurate and another disagreed. An audit of the quiz side found:

- **The exact Legend is hard to hit.** A Player sitting exactly on a Build gets that Legend #1 about 69% of the time and in the top 3 about 97%. The #1 result is brittle: for 95% of Players, changing one answer changes it.
- **Domain Axes dominate matching.** They make up 79% of the distance between Builds, so the top 3 cluster in one Domain group even for Players who care about how a deck plays. Only 47% of those Players' top-3 slots show their Archetype, against 87% if we matched on playstyle alone.
- **The Domain Questions measure temperament** ("fired up", "rules are there to be bent") instead of what each Domain's cards do. They also overlap with the pace and variance Axes.
- **Two-answer scenarios push Players to extremes.** 15 of the 18 scenarios have two answers, so a mild lean scores like a strong one.
- **Some Questions have a "correct" answer**, all statements push low when agreed with, Questions run in Axis blocks, and nothing asks about the choices that separate Builds of one Legend.
- **Opposite-Domain Legends attract neutral Players.** Akali, Jayce and Kennen sit at the Domain midpoint, so Players with no Domain preference land on them.
- **The favourite-champion bonus (3) outweighs the typical gap between #1 and #2**, and shared links can preview a different top Legend from the one the recipient sees.

## Workstreams

Grouped by the part of the system they touch. Each group can be shipped and measured on its own.

### A. Matching engine (`src/lib/scoring.ts`)

1. Match Domains by the Player's affinity for each of the Legend's two Domains, at weight 0.5 against playstyle. The top Matches are then the decks that play like you, and the Domain-lean section serves Players who choose by Domain. This replaced a plain reweighting because reweighting alone left Akali, Jayce and Kennen attracting balanced Players.
2. Score opposite-pair Legends by the nearer of their two Domains, with the other counted as neutral. The stored midpoint and validation stay unchanged.
3. Cut the favourite-champion bonus to 1 so it acts as a tie-break. Build share links without it so the preview matches what the recipient sees.
4. Only name a Domain in the Domain-lean section when the Player's score on that Axis is at least 2.5 either way.

ADR 0004 records A1 to A3.

### B. Answer formats (schema, UI)

1. Add a strength scale for two-pole scenarios: Definitely / Leaning / Leaning / Definitely, with no midpoint. The strong points keep the original Answer ids.
2. Show a close-call line on the result when #1 and #2 are within a margin tuned to fire for 15–25% of Players.

### C. Question content (`src/data/questions.json`, version 2026-10)

Redesign from first principles. Each current Question is kept, rewritten or cut, and new kinds of Question are considered. Targets:

1. Domain Questions measure mechanics, with at most one flavour item per Domain Axis and ideally four items per Domain Axis.
2. Add invented-card choices and deck-slot choices ("add one" / "cut one") aimed at what separates Builds.
3. Every option should read as a legitimate, skilled choice.
4. Balance keying and which pole is listed first, interleave the Axes, and keep eyebrows Axis-neutral.
5. Cut cross-loads the Legend pool doesn't have. Move the complexity split up to the pool median.

The rationale is in docs/research/2026-09-23-question-redesign.md.

### D. Validation

1. Add a repeatable simulation, `pnpm simulate`, covering Build recovery, Archetype share for playstyle-first Players, Domain match for Domain-first Players, centre-Legend share, brittleness and the close-call rate.
2. Build a test-player panel of 8 fictional Players (docs/eval/test-players.md). An expert picks the Legends that should suit each Player without seeing the quiz. AI role-players take the baseline and v2 quizzes as each Player and then rate their results.
3. Re-check the persona fixtures against v2.

### Deferred

Each needs A–C to land first, or belongs to another process.

- Adaptive tie-break Questions when #1 and #2 are close. These change the fixed Question set, so they need an ADR and "asked-only" reach in scoring.
- A "What drove this" line on each Match, showing the Answers that pulled the Player toward it.
- Showing near-duplicate Builds together, or re-rating them further apart: Ambessa and Sett, Jax and Master Yi, Rek'Sai and Vi. This belongs to Ingestion.
- Pool gaps: there is no Calm or Mind Aggro, and no Calm Combo. Reweighting can't fix these; only new Legends can.

## Results

See docs/research/2026-09-23-quiz-v2-evaluation.md. Simulation criteria are met: Build recovery top-3 96–97%, playstyle-first share 60%, opposite-pair share 2%, and the neighbour-slip top-1 rose from 35% to 70%. On the panel, the median rank of the expert's picks went from 12 to 5, and role-players rated their #1 4.25 against 3.50. The top-3 and overall ratings stayed flat. The evaluation ranks the open gaps: clamp extreme Profiles when matching, separate combo from luck on variance, improve the weak #3 slot, and give flavour a place.

## Success criteria (v2 compared with baseline)

- **Simulation:** Build recovery top-3 stays at 93% or better. For playstyle-first Players, own-Archetype share of top-3 slots rises from 47% to 60% or more. Akali, Jayce and Kennen take no more than 3× their fair share of #1 results under random clicking. Single-answer brittleness falls.
- **Test panel:** more expert-picked Legends appear in the top 3, fewer "bad fit" Legends appear there, and the role-players' agreement ratings go up.
