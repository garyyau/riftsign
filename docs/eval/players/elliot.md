## 16. Elliot Byrne

They'd rather you wonder what's face-down than know what's coming.

**Background.** Eight months into Riftbound. They play weekly at a store and in a small Discord league. They played Android: Netrunner for years, and they love Coup and Inscryption. They rarely play LoL, and when they do it's Teemo or Shaco, "for the traps". They play to win, and their favourite part is an opponent making the wrong read.

**How they describe themselves.** "I'm a control player, but the fun part is the hidden stuff. I like having a face-down card and letting you guess. Half my wins are the opponent playing around something I didn't have."

**How they actually play.** Accurate. Hidden cards are their favourite thing in the game, from Mind and Chaos alike, and they like Calm's reactions too. A typical turn is to set a Hidden card, leave mana open, and pass. They rarely attack first and are happy with long games. They like lots of decisions, especially ones about what the opponent thinks they have. They take bluffing risks, like leaving mana up with nothing behind it, but they dislike random effects. To them a bluff is a decision and a random effect isn't. They enjoy Chaos's discard only when they get to pick the card. When they're behind they bluff harder and wait for the opponent to overextend. They run few units and lots of spells and Hidden cards. Unlike most tricksters, they aren't in a hurry. They'll spend six turns making you nervous before they do anything.

**What would feel wrong.** A deck where everything is face-up. An aggro deck, or a deck of big units that wins by being bigger. A straight draw-and-answer control deck with no Hidden cards would also miss the point. It would win the way they like to win, but without the fun part.
