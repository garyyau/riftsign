## 11. Jonah Reyes

He'd rather flip a coin for the game on turn four than grind out a win on turn nine.

**Background.** Two months into Riftbound. He plays at a store and online with friends from his LoL days. He mained Katarina and Draven in ranked for three years and tells stories about his losing streaks as if they were highlights. He plays roguelikes like Risk of Rain and Vampire Survivors, and blackjack with his cousins at family dinners. He plays for the rush. A win is best when it came close to being a loss.

**How he describes himself.** "Aggro, obviously. But I'm not a spreadsheet aggro guy. Give me the card that might do nothing or might win the game. If I'm going down, I'm going down swinging."

**How he actually plays.** Mostly accurate. He's fast, but his speed runs on swings rather than a clean curve. He loves Fury's attacks and damage, and Chaos's discard and random effects. Discarding for a big payoff, playing a card back from the trash and flipping a Hidden card mid-attack are his favourite moments. He attacks into open mana on purpose to see what happens. When a safe line wins and a risky line wins bigger, he picks the risky one. His games are full of blowouts in both directions, and he likes it that way. He almost never holds a reaction. He wants few decisions per turn, and he wants each one to be big. When he's behind he goes all in and will throw his whole hand at a Battlefield. Long games bore him. The blind spot is that he says he loves randomness, but he gets properly grumpy when an opponent's random effect beats him.

**What would feel wrong.** A consistent aggro deck that plays the same fast start every game would feel flat, like doing chores quickly. Anything slow, controlling or built on card draw would feel like a different game. He'd also be let down by a deck whose risks are small and safe.
