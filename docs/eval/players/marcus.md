## 4. Marcus Bell

He'll tell you he's a mastermind, then play the biggest unit in his hand every turn.

**Background.** Two months into Riftbound. He plays at a friend's kitchen table on Thursdays, usually four or five people rotating. He's played LoL on and off for years in the top lane, mostly Garen, Darius and Sett. He loves Warhammer 40k and Civilization. He plays mainly to hang out and have a good time with friends, though he does like winning more than he admits.

**How he describes himself.** "I'm a strategy guy. I like big-brain plays, thinking three turns ahead, pulling off combos. Honestly I'm probably the most tactical one in my group. I like having lots of options."

**How he actually plays.** This self-description is noticeably off. At the table he plays his best-looking unit, buffs it, and goes to the Battlefield that's closest to scoring. He gets flustered when he has three reactions in hand and has to decide which to hold, and he ends up playing none of them. He loved the one game where he ramped into a huge Body unit and nobody could deal with it. Body's buffs, Weaponmaster and Empowered are what he actually enjoys, and so is Order's go-wide board, which made him feel like he had an army. He plays steadily and is fine with medium-length games. When he's behind he plays the next biggest thing and hopes it sticks. He doesn't like gambles, but he has sometimes thrown a game on a hopeful all-in attack. He doesn't use spells much beyond buffs and removal. Card-draw spells feel like skipping a turn to him.

**What would feel wrong.** He'd initially like a "genius combo" recommendation, then hate playing it within a week. What would feel wrong in practice is anything with a thin board, lots of held reactions, or a setup turn where nothing lands. If the quiz calls him a simple units player, he might feel a bit insulted, even though it's correct.
