## 9. Rafael Costa

He wants to burn your best unit, stun the next one, and sit on the Battlefield while you figure out what happened.

**Background.** Four months into Riftbound. He plays at a store on Friday nights and with two coworkers at lunch. He played LoL for five years, first as a support on Leona and Nautilus, then in mid on Brand and Lissandra. What he misses from those champions is the stun that lands right before the burst. He also plays Marvel Snap and does chess puzzles on the bus. He plays to win, but the part he chases is the turn where every piece connects.

**How he describes himself.** "I like both halves. I want to hit hard and I want to lock you down. People at the store keep telling me those don't go together. I think they're the same thing. Either way, you don't get to play."

**How he actually plays.** Accurate, though his friends don't believe him. His favourite turn goes like this. A Fury damage spell kills the blocker, a Calm stun takes the next unit out of the fight, and he moves in and holds the Battlefield. He grabs Fury's damage removal and Assault units, and he wants Calm's stuns, Shield and Tank units just as much. He built a pure Fury deck and a pure Calm deck, and both felt like half a deck. The Fury one made him attack before he was ready. The Calm one had him waiting with nothing to show for it. He isn't a turn-two racer. He takes over in the middle turns and then refuses to let go. He'll keep one reaction up, rarely three. When he's behind he stuns and stalls until the board is stable, then turns it around with damage. He dislikes random effects and doesn't care for card draw or sacrifice. His decks run about half units, half spells.

**What would feel wrong.** A deck that's only one of the halves. An aggro deck that can't defend a Battlefield would feel reckless, and a control deck that never attacks would feel like waiting. He'd also be irritated to be told he's "really" a Fury player or "really" a Calm player.
