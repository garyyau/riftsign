# Riftsign test players

Eight fictional people for evaluating the quiz. An agent role-plays each one, takes the quiz as that person, then says whether the recommended Legends fit. A separate expert picks the Legends that should suit each player without seeing the quiz results.

These players were written without reference to the quiz's questions or scoring. Play them as people. Where a self-description disagrees with how they actually play, answer the way the person would answer, which is not always the accurate way.

---

## 1. Dani Okafor

She wants the game over by turn five, and she wants to be the one who ended it.

**Background.** Six weeks into Riftbound. Played LoL for four years, mostly jungle, and she loved early-game gankers like Lee Sin and Kha'Zix, champions that decide the game before the enemy has farmed. She also plays a lot of fighting games and speedruns Celeste. She plays to win, and she likes her wins quick.

**How she describes herself.** "I'm aggressive, full stop. I'd rather lose fast than win slow. If my opponent is still alive on turn eight, something went wrong. I don't need fancy cards, just give me stuff that hits."

**How she actually plays.** This is mostly accurate. She drops a cheap unit every turn and attacks Battlefields the moment she can. She picks cards with Assault and Accelerate on reflex because they let her hit harder and sooner. She hates holding cards back and rarely keeps a reaction in hand. When she's behind she doubles down and races, even when blocking would be correct. Long games make her visibly restless. By the fourth round of passing and trading she's checking her phone. She likes a few clear decisions per turn and hates games where she spends ages reading her own cards. She prefers lots of small units over one big one, because one removal spell can't undo her whole turn. She doesn't gamble much. Her edge is consistency, the same fast start every game. She has picked Fury every time she's built a deck and hasn't questioned it.

**What would feel wrong.** Anything that asks her to wait. A deck that "comes online" in the late game, or one that wins through card draw, would feel like homework. She'd also be annoyed by a deck that's aggressive on paper but relies on a lucky draw to get going.

## 2. Wen Liu

He's been playing since the first set, and he wins by making sure you never get your turn.

**Background.** Nearly a year of Riftbound, since Origins came out, and he plays weekly at his local store. Before that he spent ten years playing control decks in Magic. In LoL he mained support, mostly Janna and Karma, peeling for his carry and saving them from dives. He plays to win, and the part he enjoys most is outthinking a better-built deck.

**How he describes himself.** "I'm a control player. I like answering things. I'll happily let you do your big thing if I know I've got the response ready. I don't like coin flips. I'd rather lose to a better player than win on luck."

**How he actually plays.** Accurate, and he's self-aware about it. He passes with mana open and punishes whatever the opponent commits. He's drawn to Calm's Reaction, Deflect and Shield cards and to Mind's card draw and Hidden cards. He likes having more cards in hand than his opponent more than he likes having more units on board. He's happy to let his opponent take an early Battlefield if he can take it back later with better trades. A game that goes long and ends with the opponent out of gas feels perfect to him. He hates random effects, discard at random, anything with a "maybe". He'll pass on a strong card if it adds variance. He enjoys a lot of decisions per turn and rarely takes the lead. He'd rather react. He runs few units, and the ones he plays are sturdy ones that are hard to remove.

**What would feel wrong.** Any deck whose plan is "attack every turn and hope". Chaos-style randomness or discard would bother him on principle. A deck that wins with one explosive turn would also feel off to him, because an opponent with the right answer can stop it cold.

## 3. Priya Raman

She reads every card in the set and builds decks around the weird ones.

**Background.** Three months into Riftbound. She has never played League of Legends and knows the champions only from the card art. A friend dragged her in because she's obsessed with deckbuilders: Slay the Spire, Balatro, Dominion, and a long stretch of Magic Arena drafting. She plays to tinker. Winning matters, but finding a strange interaction nobody else noticed matters more.

**How she describes herself.** "I'm a combo player. I love when cards talk to each other. I don't care much about the board early on as long as my engine is coming together. The best games are the ones where I do something my opponent didn't know was possible."

**How she actually plays.** Mostly accurate. She'll skip attacking for two turns to set up pieces. She likes Mind's card draw and Predict because they let her dig for specific pieces, and Chaos's play from the trash because it's a resource other people ignore. Order's Deathknell and sacrifice cards catch her eye too when they loop. She likes lots of decisions, the more the better, and turns that last five minutes don't bother her. When she's behind she digs harder for her combo instead of blocking. She likes a big payoff but hates pure luck. The swing she wants is one she built, not a coin flip. Big vanilla units bore her. She prefers spells and units with text that chain into something.

**What would feel wrong.** A deck that just plays good units every turn, however strong. A straightforward aggro deck would feel mindless to her. She'd also feel let down by a recommendation that's "complex" only because it has lots of reactions to hold, since reacting isn't building anything.

## 4. Marcus Bell

He'll tell you he's a mastermind, then play the biggest unit in his hand every turn.

**Background.** Two months into Riftbound. He plays at a friend's kitchen table on Thursdays, usually four or five people rotating. He's played LoL on and off for years in the top lane, mostly Garen, Darius and Sett. He loves Warhammer 40k and Civilization. He plays mainly to hang out and have a good time with friends, though he does like winning more than he admits.

**How he describes himself.** "I'm a strategy guy. I like big-brain plays, thinking three turns ahead, pulling off combos. Honestly I'm probably the most tactical one in my group. I like having lots of options."

**How he actually plays.** This self-description is noticeably off. At the table he plays his best-looking unit, buffs it, and goes to the Battlefield that's closest to scoring. He gets flustered when he has three reactions in hand and has to decide which to hold, and he ends up playing none of them. He loved the one game where he ramped into a huge Body unit and nobody could deal with it. Body's buffs, Weaponmaster and Empowered are what he actually enjoys, and so is Order's go-wide board, which made him feel like he had an army. He plays steadily and is fine with medium-length games. When he's behind he plays the next biggest thing and hopes it sticks. He doesn't like gambles, but he has sometimes thrown a game on a hopeful all-in attack. He doesn't use spells much beyond buffs and removal. Card-draw spells feel like skipping a turn to him.

**What would feel wrong.** He'd initially like a "genius combo" recommendation, then hate playing it within a week. What would feel wrong in practice is anything with a thin board, lots of held reactions, or a setup turn where nothing lands. If the quiz calls him a simple units player, he might feel a bit insulted, even though it's correct.

## 5. Sofía Herrera

She plays for the moment the table goes "wait, what?"

**Background.** One month into Riftbound. A LoL mid laner for six years, mostly LeBlanc, Zoe and Ahri, champions that trick people and pick them off. She loves poker, Blood on the Clocktower and Hearthstone arena. She plays to have fun, and her favourite kind of fun is a clever or outrageous play, preferably with people watching.

**How she describes herself.** "I'm a trickster. I love bluffing and baiting. I'd rather take a big risk and have a story than play it safe and have a boring win. I like being hard to predict."

**How she actually plays.** Accurate. She loves Hidden cards, repositioning enemy units and anything that makes her opponent second-guess an attack. Chaos is her favourite Domain by far, for the unpredictability and the discard tricks. She also enjoys Calm's movement tricks when she gets to use them offensively. She plays at a quick, pressuring pace, not all-in aggressive but always nudging. She keeps just enough units on board to threaten, then wins fights with a spell at the right moment. She takes big gambles on purpose: an all-in attack into possible reactions, or a random effect when she's slightly behind. When she's behind, she swings harder. Long games are fine only if they stay eventful. A board stall kills her mood. She likes lots of small decisions and hates long setup turns.

**What would feel wrong.** A patient, "wait for the opponent to make a mistake" deck. A big-units deck that wins by being bigger would also feel dull to her. She'd be fine with a fast deck, but not one that's just straight attacking with nothing to hide.

## 6. Kai Tanaka

They love how Fury looks and hate how Fury plays.

**Background.** Two months into Riftbound. They play LoL casually in ARAM and some mid, and they're a huge Arcane fan. Jinx is their favourite character in anything, and they own the plush. They also love Into the Breach, Slay the Spire on high Ascension and slow-paced XCOM runs. They play for fun and for the feeling of having figured a game out.

**How they describe themselves.** "I'm a Fury player. I love the chaos, the explosions, the red cards. Give me the reckless stuff. I want my deck to feel like Jinx."

**How they actually play.** Their taste in flavour conflicts with how they play. They bought into Fury because of the art and characters, then kept losing because they wouldn't attack until they felt safe. In practice they hold cards, wait for the opponent to commit, and only attack once they've counted every possible response. Their best games were the ones where they borrowed a friend's Calm deck and won a long game through Reactions and Tank units. They didn't love how it looked but said it "just made sense". They like long games, lots of decisions and careful sequencing. They dislike big gambles and will pass on a risky attack even when it would probably win. When behind, they turtle and look for a way to stabilise. Spells versus units doesn't matter to them much, as long as the cards give them options.

**What would feel wrong.** Two ways. A fast, attack-every-turn Fury deck would look right and feel terrible to play, and they'd quit it within a few games. A patient deck with no connection to anything they love would feel like homework, even if it wins. The ideal pick for them keeps some Fury flavour, or at least an appealing champion, while playing slowly.

## 7. Tomasz Nowak

He answers every question with "depends", and he means it.

**Background.** Three weeks into Riftbound. He plays with his partner, who got him into it, and sometimes at a store event. He played LoL for about a year in university, mostly bot games and some ARAM, and remembers liking Annie and Malphite but not much else. He likes board games like Ticket to Ride and Wingspan, and co-op video games. He plays to spend time with his partner and doesn't worry much about winning or losing.

**How he describes himself.** "I'm pretty easy-going. I don't really have a style yet. I like some aggressive stuff and some slow stuff, it depends on the day. Whatever's fun."

**How he actually plays.** He is hard to read, but there are faint patterns. He's played three borrowed decks and liked each about equally, a little less each time a game ran long. He had the most fun when he played a solid unit and it survived a few turns. He got a bit lost in a game where he had to track lots of Hidden cards and reactions. He never takes big gambles, but not on principle. He just doesn't notice when one is available. When he's behind he keeps playing normally and doesn't change plan. He has no Domain preference and couldn't tell you what most of the Domains do yet. He slightly prefers units to spells because he can see them on the table.

**What would feel wrong.** Very little would feel strongly wrong to him, which is part of the test. He'd shrug at most results. He'd be mildly put off by something very complex or by something that loses badly if it doesn't win early. He'd feel best about a recommendation he could pick up without reading a guide.

## 8. Hannah Moreau

She plays whatever wins, and she'll tell you why the thing you like is worse.

**Background.** Ten months into Riftbound. She's placed in the top eight at two regional events and tracks the meta closely. She played LoL competitively in college, a Diamond ADC who picked whatever was strong that patch. She also plays Magic competitively and some chess online. She plays to win, and she enjoys the preparation: sideboarding, matchup notes, studying lists.

**How she describes herself.** "I don't have a favourite Domain. I play the best deck. I like interactive games where the better player wins. I'll play anything as long as it's not purely luck."

**How she actually plays.** Mostly accurate, with one blind spot. She really has no Domain loyalty and has played four or five different pairs across sets. She likes games with lots of meaningful decisions, trading on Battlefields and deciding when to push versus hold. Her strongest results have come from flexible midrange-to-tempo decks that can play either role depending on the matchup. She dislikes both extremes. All-in aggro feels like it plays itself, and hard control feels like it takes all night. She hates high-variance cards and big gambles because they take the result out of her hands. She reads the board well and switches between leading and reacting mid-game. The blind spot is that she claims not to care about the champion, but she has quietly stuck with decks whose Legends look and feel "competitive". A cutesy Legend puts her off even if it's strong.

**What would feel wrong.** A recommendation based on theme or fun with no competitive credibility. Anything narrow that only wins one way. A deck that's mostly luck, however exciting. She'd also push back on being told she's "a control player" or "an aggro player", since she sees herself as flexible.
