# Riftsign eval ground truth

Expert picks for the eight players in `test-players.md`. I judged each player on how they actually play and what would feel wrong to them, not on how they describe themselves. Favourite champions only broke ties. The picks come from the reviewed Legend catalogue and never looked at the quiz's data or scoring.

Machine-readable copy: `%TEMP%\riftsign-eval\ground-truth.json`.

---

## Dani Okafor

**Key.** Archetype matters most. She needs fast, go-wide aggro with a consistent start. Late-game engines and draw-dependent or gambling aggro are out.

**Best**
1. Renekton, Butcher of the Sands (Aggro). Unit after unit, every spare rune turned into another body, and nothing to hold back. That's her whole game.
2. Rengar, Pridestalker (Aggro). A wave of cheap Fury units that hit harder than they look, so one removal spell can't undo her turn.
3. Jinx, Loose Cannon (Aggro). She gets to dump her hand and get refilled, and she hates holding cards anyway. I ranked it below the other two because Chaos adds a little variance.

**Good.** Darius, Ambessa, Annie, Sett, Vi, Rek'Sai.

**Bad**
- Nasus. Surviving until turn eight or nine is exactly the "waiting" she hates.
- Jhin. The long fuse and slow setup would feel like homework to her.
- Ezreal. This deck wins by outlasting on card draw, and she'd be checking her phone by round four.

## Wen Liu

**Key.** He needs a Control archetype with low variance. Calm reactions plus Mind card advantage is the ideal pair. Chaos Legends only qualify if the Build itself has no randomness.

**Best**
1. Ahri, Nine-Tailed Fox (Control). It's Calm/Mind, his two Domains. He lets the opponent commit, punishes the attack, and grinds out a lead.
2. Lux, Lady of Luminosity (Control). He holds the answer for the overextension, and every big spell refills his hand, so he never runs dry.
3. Vex, Gloomist (Control). He holds a spot with answers and draws an extra card every turn, which means more cards in hand than the opponent. Its Chaos cards are bounce and stun, not coin flips.

**Good.** Viktor, Shen, Ezreal, Kai'Sa (Control), Poppy, Irelia.

**Bad**
- Zed. It's a gamble-and-race aggro deck that runs on random discard, the two things he rejects on principle.
- Renata Glasc. One explosive turn with a swingy build-up, which is the kind of plan he says a right answer stops cold.
- Jinx. Attack every turn and hope.

## Priya Raman

**Key.** She needs an engine she builds herself: trash as a resource, Deathknell and sacrifice loops, cards that talk to each other. Domain matters less. Complexity has to come from synergy, not from holding reactions.

**Best**
1. Kennen, Heart of the Tempest (Tempo). Playing from the trash and other zones to charge up a payoff, in Order/Chaos, hits two of her stated interests at once.
2. LeBlanc, Deceiver (Midrange). She copies units that die on purpose into Deathknell payoffs. That's the kind of loop she hunts for.
3. Jhin, Virtuoso (Combo). She stockpiles banished spells and cashes them in for one turn she built herself, not one she lucked into.

**Good.** Jayce, Renata Glasc, Sivir, Yasuo, Ivern, Miss Fortune.

**Bad**
- Renekton. It just plays units every turn, the "mindless" deck she describes.
- Rumble. It sells itself on doing one clear thing with no tricky sequences, and she'd be bored within a game.
- Shen. All of its complexity is holding reactions, which she says isn't building anything.

## Marcus Bell

**Key.** Match how he actually plays, not what he says. He wants a big board of Body and Order units with buffs, and no held reactions, draw spells or dead setup turns. He'll like the result more if it sounds strategic.

**Best**
1. Garen, Might of Demacia (Midrange). It's a wide army that draws off conquering instead of draw spells. Garen is also his main.
2. Fiora, Grand Duelist (Midrange). He equips and buffs units past five Might. That's Weaponmaster play, and its "plan a turn ahead" framing flatters his self-image.
3. Sett, The Boss (Tempo). He commits strong Body units, buffs them, and saves the buffed one from a bad trade. Another champion he loves.

**Good.** Ambessa, Volibear, Lucian, Leona, Rumble, Darius.

**Bad**
- Jhin. Slow setup turns where nothing lands, then a combo he'd abandon within a week.
- Ezreal. Removal and card draw with a thin board is everything he finds tedious.
- Ahri. It's a reactive grind where he'd freeze with three reactions in hand.

## Sofía Herrera

**Key.** She wants Chaos, Hidden cards and mid-fight tricks at a pressuring tempo pace. Patient control and "be bigger" unit plans are both wrong for her.

**Best**
1. Teemo, Swift Scout (Tempo). A row of face-down bluffs, an early lead, then ambushes. It's her self-description in a deck.
2. Diana, Scorn of the Moon (Tempo). A few units, cheap spells, and a trick at exactly the right second, with bouncing of enemy units on top.
3. Draven, Glorious Executioner (Midrange). Fury/Chaos combat tricks mid-fight and cards kept face down, with every fight a live decision.

**Good.** Annie, Yasuo, Kha'Zix, Zed, Kennen, Rengar.

**Bad**
- Shen. It's the patient "wait for them to make a mistake" deck she named.
- Volibear. It wins by being bigger after a quiet start.
- Poppy. A long grind with no excitement per turn, which means a board stall.

## Kai Tanaka

**Key.** Play pattern beats their self-description. They want patient, reactive, careful sequencing and no gambles. Flavour decides between otherwise equal picks, so a Fury Domain or an Arcane champion helps.

**Best**
1. Kai'Sa, Daughter of the Void (Control). It's slow and reactive with holding runes open, and it's still a Fury Legend, so it keeps the red cards they love.
2. Akali, Rogue Assassin (Midrange). It's Fury/Calm. It opens careful, holds answers, and only pushes once it's safe, which matches how they won with a friend's Calm deck.
3. Viktor, Herald of the Arcane (Control). He's an Arcane character, and the plan is patient removal that tips a long game one answer at a time.

**Good.** Jhin, Volibear, Jayce, Shen, Pyke, Vex.

**Bad**
- Jinx. It looks perfect and plays exactly the way they say they'd quit.
- Zed. A Fury race built on taking chances.
- Renekton. Constant attacking with nothing held back.

## Tomasz Nowak

**Key.** He needs low complexity and visible units that stick around. Domain doesn't matter. He should be able to pick it up without reading a guide, and it shouldn't lose badly if it doesn't win early.

**Best**
1. Garen, Might of Demacia (Midrange). It's a starter deck built to teach fundamentals: a wide board he can see.
2. Rumble, Mechanized Menace (Midrange). It's billed as a comfortable learning deck. Sturdy Mechs survive on defence, and he has one plan to follow.
3. Lee Sin, Blind Monk (Midrange). Another starter deck. He buffs one solid unit and watches it survive and grow, which is the game he enjoyed most.

**Good.** Master Yi (Wuju Bladesman), Fiora, Lucian, Ornn, Leona, Annie.

**Bad**
- Teemo. It asks him to track many Hidden cards, exactly where he got lost.
- Yasuo. The Build openly asks a lot of the player in exchange for flashy plays.
- Kennen. It's spell-heavy with cards played from several zones, too much to read for someone three weeks in.

## Hannah Moreau

**Key.** She wants flexible midrange-to-tempo decks that can lead or react, with low variance. The Legend also has to look competitive, so cutesy champions are out even if strong.

**Best**
1. Akali, Rogue Assassin (Midrange). It rewards reading the game over following a fixed script, and it switches between trading and pushing mid-game.
2. Kai'Sa, Daughter of the Void (Midrange). She reads the matchup and chooses safe removal or pressure, and resources keep her decisions open every turn.
3. Kha'Zix, Voidreaver (Midrange). Every Battlefield fight is a press-or-retreat call, and it's won by small edges rather than gambles.

**Good.** Draven, Azir, Diana, Leona, LeBlanc, Jax.

**Bad**
- Zed. It's luck-driven aggro that plays itself.
- Renata Glasc. A swingy one-way combo that "can swing hard in either direction".
- Teemo. It's a cute Yordle trickster, the kind of Legend her blind spot rejects.
